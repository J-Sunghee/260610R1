# Peer Review Report — reviewer (ILE 기준)

**Journal**: Interactive Learning Environments (Taylor & Francis)
**Manuscript Title**: Comparable Comprehension, Divergent Learning Experiences: Comparing Human-Made and AI-Generated Educational Podcasts in Higher Education
**Review Date**: April 28, 2026

---

## 1. Summary

This manuscript presents a mixed-methods investigation comparing human-made and AI-generated educational podcasts across three dimensions: interactional features (via Conversation Analysis), content comprehension, and subjective learning experience. The study employs a two-study convergent design — a counterbalanced within-subjects experiment (n=52) and a course-embedded qualitative inquiry (n=9) — supplemented by CA of four podcast transcripts. The central finding is that while comprehension outcomes are statistically equivalent across podcast types, learners report significantly more positive learning experiences with human-made podcasts (Cohen's d=.36), and CA reveals substantive differences in turn-taking structure, repair sequences, and epistemic positioning despite surface-level acoustic similarities. The authors propose "interactional presence" as a theoretical construct and suggest differential activation thresholds between cognitive and affective-social processing channels. The manuscript addresses a timely and practically relevant question; however, it requires substantial revision before it is suitable for publication, particularly with respect to theoretical integration, construct validity, methodological transparency, and the empirical grounding of its key theoretical contributions.

---

## 2. Evaluation by Criterion

### A. Significance & Originality
**Score: 4 / 5**

**Strengths**: The research question is genuinely timely and practically significant. As generative AI tools (e.g., NotebookLM) enter higher education workflows at scale, systematic evidence comparing human-made and AI-generated instructional media is urgently needed. The combination of CA with experimental and qualitative methods is methodologically creative and relatively rare in the ILE literature. The proposal of "interactional presence" as a distinct theoretical construct represents a meaningful conceptual contribution. The manuscript fits squarely within ILE's scope on technology-mediated learning environments.

**Weaknesses**: The novelty of the "interactional presence" construct and the "differential activation thresholds" hypothesis is asserted rather than rigorously derived. The paper's contribution would be substantially strengthened if the authors situated these proposals more explicitly against the existing literature on parasocial interaction, presence theory, and social presence in online learning. As currently framed, it is unclear whether "interactional presence" is a new construct or a relabeling of existing concepts.

---

### B. Theoretical Grounding
**Score: 3 / 5**

**Strengths**: The selection of four theoretical frameworks — institutional talk, the Voice Principle, Cognitive Load Theory, and Social Agency Theory — is well-motivated. Their combination is appropriate for the research questions posed. The use of Drew and Heritage's (1992) institutional talk as an analytical lens for CA is methodologically sound.

**Weaknesses**:

1. **Fragmentation and missing integration**: The four frameworks are introduced in parallel but never synthesized into a coherent theoretical model prior to data collection. A theoretical model or conceptual diagram linking the frameworks to specific predictions for each RQ would substantially improve this section.

2. **Construct validity of "interactional presence"**: This central theoretical contribution is introduced in the Discussion section without prior theoretical scaffolding. The construct's dimensions are listed but not operationalized or distinguished from related constructs (social presence, parasocial interaction, anthropomorphism).

3. **Voice Principle citation**: The paper cites Fiorella and Mayer (2022) for the Voice Principle, but the canonical Voice Principle research predates this citation (Mayer et al., 2003). The nature of the 2022 update should be specified.

4. **Social Agency Theory application**: Social Agency Theory (Mayer et al., 2003) was developed in the context of on-screen agents, not audio. A more careful discussion of whether and how the theory's mechanisms transfer to the audio-only modality is needed.

---

### C. Methodological Rigor
**Score: 3 / 5**

**Strengths**: The convergent mixed-methods design is appropriate and well-justified. The counterbalanced within-subjects design for Study 1 is efficient. Power analysis is reported and appears adequate. Jefferson's (2004) transcription conventions are standard and appropriate. Internal consistency for comprehension measures (α=.828/.799) and the learning experience scale (α=.925) is acceptable.

**Weaknesses**:

1. **CA sampling rationale**: The CA is performed on four podcast episodes, but the basis for selecting specific sequences is not stated. Without this, it is unclear whether analyzed excerpts are representative.

2. **Comprehension instrument validity**: No evidence of content validity, construct validity, or test-retest reliability is provided. Alpha coefficients indicate internal consistency only.

3. **Learning experience scale**: The nature and extent of adaptation from Netland and Christ (2024) are not described. The construct measured must be defined with greater precision.

4. **Instructor-researcher role conflict in Study 2**: No procedural safeguards are described to mitigate researcher bias (e.g., member checking, independent coding, respondent validation).

5. **Content analysis coding procedure**: Coding scheme, category definitions, inter-rater reliability (IRR), and coder training are not described.

6. **Topic equivalence**: The two topics (Social Comparison Theory, Quantum Computing) differ substantially in prior knowledge demands, and the absence of a full factorial design means topic effects cannot be separated from podcast-type effects.

7. **Statistical analysis choice**: Paired and independent samples t-tests are used. A mixed ANOVA or multilevel model would be more appropriate for the within-subjects design.

---

### D. Quality of Results
**Score: 3 / 5**

**Strengths**: Effect sizes (Cohen's d) are reported for the learning experience comparison. CA findings are presented with transcript excerpts. Both quantitative and qualitative findings are presented clearly.

**Weaknesses**:

1. **Missing confidence intervals**: p-values are reported but not confidence intervals for mean differences.

2. **Effect size for comprehension null results**: The paper does not report effect sizes for the null comprehension comparisons. A null result with d=.05 has very different theoretical implications from one with d=.30 that is underpowered.

3. **CA depth and systematicity**: For features such as "repair absence," a claim of systematic absence requires demonstrating that contexts where repair would be relevant are present but unrepaired in AI podcasts — this analysis is not clearly presented.

4. **Preference data**: Presented as descriptive frequencies without statistical analysis.

5. **Convergence across studies**: The Discussion does not systematically address the degree of convergence or divergence between Study 1 and Study 2 findings.

---

### E. Discussion & Implications
**Score: 3 / 5**

**Strengths**: The discussion makes a genuine effort to connect findings to theoretical frameworks and propose practical implications. The identification of a dissociation between comprehension and learning experience is theoretically interesting. The extension of the CASTLE framework (Schneider et al., 2022) is a useful practical contribution.

**Weaknesses**:

1. **"Differential activation thresholds" hypothesis**: This is stated as a conclusion rather than as a hypothesis requiring further empirical testing. The manuscript should clearly distinguish between what the data support and what is speculative theoretical extension.

2. **Alternative explanations insufficiently addressed**: Comprehension equivalence can be explained by test insensitivity, content equivalence, or carryover effects — these alternatives are not systematically addressed.

3. **Learning experience construct conflation**: "Learning experience" is treated as unitary but appears to tap engagement, perceived authenticity, enjoyment, and social connection simultaneously.

4. **Generalizability claims**: Conclusions about "educational podcasts" broadly should be more carefully qualified given the specific populations studied (Korean undergraduates; U.S. graduate students).

---

### F. Writing Quality
**Score: 3 / 5**

**Strengths**: The manuscript is generally well-organized. Technical CA vocabulary is used with appropriate precision. Tables and transcript excerpts are well-formatted.

**Weaknesses**:

1. **APA 7th edition compliance**: Several citation formatting issues are apparent. The manuscript should be reviewed systematically (see literature-checker report for details).

2. **Construct terminology inconsistency**: "Interactional presence," "social agency," "learning experience," and "engagement" appear to be used interchangeably in some passages.

3. **Transition between studies**: The logic for why Study 2 was needed in addition to Study 1 is not explicit in the Methods section.

4. **Hedging of speculative claims**: Several theoretical proposals are stated with more certainty than the data warrant.

---

### G. Educational Relevance
**Score: 4 / 5**

**Strengths**: The practical relevance to educators considering AI-generated instructional media is high and immediate. The finding that comprehension is preserved while learning experience suffers provides actionable guidance. The attention to both outcomes and experience reflects a sophisticated understanding of educational effectiveness.

**Weaknesses**: Both studies draw on specific populations; generalizability to K-12, different disciplines, or online/hybrid learning environments is not discussed. Practical recommendations are somewhat vague.

## 3. Major Concerns

1. **Theoretical coherence of "interactional presence"**: Must be differentiated from well-established constructs (social presence, parasocial interaction, anthropomorphism). Either demonstrate genuine novelty or explicitly position as a refinement of existing constructs.

2. **Missing effect sizes and CIs for null comprehension results**: Essential for evaluating the "comparable comprehension" claim. Must report Cohen's d and 95% confidence intervals for all comprehension comparisons.

3. **Inter-rater reliability for qualitative content analysis**: Must report: (a) coding scheme with category definitions; (b) whether coding was independent; (c) IRR statistics (Cohen's kappa or percentage agreement); (d) how disagreements were resolved.

4. **Comprehension instrument validation**: Must report: (a) item development process; (b) evidence items assess comprehension of podcast content vs. prior knowledge; (c) score distributions and ceiling/floor effects.

5. **Analytical model for Study 1**: A mixed-model analysis (podcast type × topic as factors) would allow testing of topic × podcast type interactions and better leverage the study design.

6. **Learning experience scale description**: The adapted scale's construct, modifications, and pilot testing must be documented, as this provides the evidence for the study's most significant finding.

---

## 4. Minor Concerns

1. **Voice Principle citation**: Include Mayer et al. (2003) as primary citation; specify the nature of the Fiorella and Mayer (2022) update.

2. **Preference data analysis**: Analyze statistically (chi-square or binomial test).

3. **Study 2 positionality statement**: Warranted given the instructor-researcher dual role.

4. **EFL listening context**: Study 1 was conducted at a Korean university — the potential impact of English as a foreign language on comprehension scores and learning experience ratings should be discussed.

5. **Convergence discussion**: A meta-inference section explicitly addressing convergence/divergence between Study 1 and Study 2 is required for a convergent mixed-methods design.

6. **NotebookLM specificity**: More clearly marked as a boundary condition; comparison with other AI podcast generators warranted in limitations.

---

## 5. Recommendation

**Decision: Major Revision**

**Justification**: This manuscript addresses a question of genuine theoretical and practical importance for ILE, and the multi-method approach is an appropriate and creative response to the complexity of the research questions. The dissociation between comprehension outcomes and learning experience quality is substantively interesting and theoretically generative.

However, the manuscript has several limitations that prevent acceptance or minor revision. Most critically: the central theoretical contribution ("interactional presence") lacks conceptual scaffolding and differentiation from existing constructs; the empirical foundation for the "comparable comprehension" claim is weakened by absent effect sizes; the qualitative content analysis lacks IRR data; and the statistical analysis does not fully exploit the study design. These concerns require additional analysis and rewriting but do not require new data collection.

The authors are encouraged to revise with particular attention to: (1) systematic theoretical positioning of "interactional presence"; (2) complete effect size and CI reporting for all comparisons; (3) full documentation of qualitative coding procedures and IRR; (4) more careful hedging of speculative theoretical proposals; and (5) a systematic convergence discussion integrating Study 1 and Study 2. A revised manuscript addressing these concerns has the potential to make a meaningful contribution to the ILE literature on AI-generated instructional media.
